# Segments, and what "one person" means in each

Cover the full range. The rule is not that every segment is equally good for a solo founder — it
is that you must know *how* each one is hard, so a flag is specific instead of vague.

| Segment | What a solo founder can realistically do | Where it breaks | First-money shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D2C physical** | One SKU, made by a contract manufacturer, sold on one channel | Inventory before revenue; returns; paid acquisition costs rising faster than margin | Pre-order or a single batch sold to a known group |
| **B2B software** | Narrow tool for one role in one industry, priced monthly | Procurement and security review at anything above small business | Three paid pilots from warm contacts |
| **SaaS self-serve** | A tool a buyer can find, try and pay for without talking to anyone | Getting found at all; support load at scale; churn invisible for months | First 20 subscribers from one community |
| **Consumer app** | A single job done better than a general tool | Distribution is the whole game and app stores are winner-take-most | Paid app or subscription from a niche audience |
| **Platform / marketplace** | Rarely solo. Only where one side is already aggregated and reachable | Cold start on two sides at once; disintermediation after first match | Manually match ten pairs by hand before building anything |
| **Productised service** | Highest solo fit. Sell the outcome, deliver with tools, fixed scope and price | It becomes a job, not a business, unless delivery is systematised early | One client at a fixed price in week two |
| **Hardware / device** | Only with an existing manufacturer and a certified-component design | Certification, tooling, warranty, and capital | Paid pilot units to one industrial buyer |
| **Infrastructure / API** | A boring, reliable piece other builders need | Long sales cycles; reliability expectations exceed one person | Usage-based, first ten developers from one ecosystem |
| **Content / media / education** | Fits solo; compounding distribution | Slow to revenue; platform-dependent | A cohort or a paid guide sold to an existing audience |
| **Local / physical operations** | A route, a location, a service area | Does not scale past the founder's hours without hiring | Ten customers in one postcode |

**When flagging an idea, use this table.** "Needs capital" is vague. "D2C physical: about Rs 4–6
lakh of stock before the first sale, and that money is at risk if the first batch does not sell"
is a flag the founder can act on.

**Sector coverage.** Rotate across runs so the output does not drift into software and
consumer convenience. Health, agriculture, construction, logistics, energy, waste, education,
financial services, manufacturing, care work, public sector, repair and maintenance, food
systems, water, insurance, legal and compliance, trades and skilled labour.

The unglamorous sectors are where a single person still has room, because the incumbents are
old, the software is bad, and nobody with a computer-science degree wants to work on it.
