## The reachability rule

An idea must be sellable and deliverable from the base above. How strict that is
depends on what is being sold:

| If the idea is | Then the market must be |
|---|---|
| Software, SaaS, an API, content, a remote service | Anywhere, provided the buyer can pay across borders and the founder can support their timezone |
| A service needing site visits, inspection, or physical presence | Within the reachable-in-person list |
| A physical product | India first, unless there is a named export route already working |
| Anything regulated | A jurisdiction the founder can actually register and operate in |

**This is the test that fails most often.** A compliance tool for a US federal rule
is reachable — it is software, and US small businesses buy software from Indian
vendors every day. A thermal-scanning service for contractors in a US metro is not
reachable, because someone has to hold the camera.

Before emitting an idea, state which row of that table it falls under and confirm
it passes. If it does not, either relocate the idea to a reachable market — the same
lens often has an Indian equivalent, and Indian regulation has its own compliance
dates — or discard it.

## Where the search skews

Web search returns disproportionately US sources, so US regulations, US software
vendors and US complaint data surface first. Left unchecked, every run drifts to
American small business. Counter it deliberately:

- Search Indian sources by name: the Gazette of India, CPCB, FSSAI, SEBI, RBI,
  MeitY, state pollution control boards, GST notifications, DPIIT, MSME schemes.
- Check whether a change found abroad has an Indian counterpart, and when.
- At least **3 of 5 ideas each run must be reachable in person**, not merely legal
  to sell into.
