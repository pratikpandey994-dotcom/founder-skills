# Required shape of every idea

Emit exactly this, in this order. No preamble, no enthusiasm, no summary paragraph at the end
beyond the handoff line. If a field would be empty, the idea is not ready — discard it.

---

### [Number]. [Plain title, no wordplay, no "AI-powered"]

**The change** — what happened, when, and the source. One or two sentences.
(Source, date)

**Who hurts** — one specific person. A role, a place, and what they are responsible for.
Not "logistics companies". "The dispatch supervisor at a 40-truck fleet in Nashik who signs off
driver hours."

**What they do today** — the current workaround, and what it costs in money or hours per month.
Label it `[Verified]`, `[Estimate]` or `[Assumption]`.

**The idea** — one sentence. What you sell, to whom, and what it replaces.

**Segment · Horizon · Lens** — e.g. B2B service · NOW · Abandoned segment

**Solo-founder fit** — the four gates, each pass or flag:
- *Build:* can one person ship something sellable in 8 weeks?
- *Sell:* can one person reach 10 paying buyers without a sales team?
- *Cash:* does money arrive before scale is needed — no cold start, no unfundable inventory?
- *Survive:* can it run for a week without the founder?

A flag is not a rejection. State what the flag costs: "needs a co-founder who can sell", "needs
about Rs X of stock before the first sale". Two or more flags means say plainly that this is not
a one-person business as described, and give the version that is.

**Execution path** — three lines, per `references/execution.md`:
- *Made by:* who manufactures or delivers it, and their minimum order or engagement. For
  software, what has to be built and in how many weeks.
- *Reaches the buyer via:* the specific channel, and what that channel takes.
- *Margin chain:* landed or delivered cost → your price → what the customer pays. If anyone
  in between takes a cut, show it. If the numbers do not leave a business, say so.

**Before the first rupee** — what must be bought or committed before any revenue arrives,
and whether the founder can afford it. This is the question that ends most ideas at this
capital level; answer it honestly rather than optimistically.

**First money** — what exactly you would sell first, to whom, at what price, within how many
weeks. This is the smallest thing someone would pay for, not the product.

**Strongest objection** — the best argument that this is wrong, made properly, not a soft
counterpoint you can wave away. If the strongest objection is fatal, say so and keep the idea in
only if the objection is testable.

**Kill signal** — one fact the founder could check **this week**, for free or nearly free, that
would end the idea. Name the exact source or the exact question to ask.

**Take it further** — `Paste into the Business Idea Validator skill: "[one-sentence idea, with
location, stage, and what you'd sell first]"`

# After the five ideas

Close with three lines and nothing more:

1. Which lenses you used this run and which you passed over for lack of a concrete change.
   Then the **cut list**: every candidate that did not survive pass two, one line each, with
   the test that killed it.
2. Which idea you would personally start on Monday, and why that one.
   Name the **domain** it sits in and say whether the domain itself is worth going deeper
   on — one good idea in a rich domain beats five scattered across unrelated ones, and a
   founder who keeps returning to the same domain compounds what they know about it.
3. The handoff: these are unvalidated starting points — run the chosen one through the Business
   Idea Validator before spending a rupee or a week.

# Self-check before you emit

Run this against your own output and fix what fails:

- Does every idea name a change with a date and a source, not a trend?
- Could a stranger find the person who hurts, from your description alone?
- Is any idea a restatement of a company that already exists at scale? Cut it.
- Is at least one idea outside software? Is at least one NEAR or COMING? Is at least one
  unglamorous? Are all five from different lenses?
- Does every kill signal cost under an hour and under a thousand rupees to check?
- Have you checked all five against the ledger for substantive repetition — not just titles?
