# Can it actually be made and sold?

An idea that names a change and a buyer is still not executable. Someone has to make the
thing, and it has to reach the person paying. Most ideas die in the gap between those two
sentences, and the gap is invisible until you cost it.

Every idea must state its **execution path**: who makes it, how it reaches the buyer, and
what is left after everyone in the chain takes their cut. If you cannot fill those in, the
idea is not ready — say so rather than dressing it up.

---

## Physical products

**You do not build a factory.** Somebody in India already makes something close to this.
Find them.

| Category | Where it is already made |
|---|---|
| Knitwear, t-shirts, innerwear | Tiruppur |
| Woven fabric, saris, synthetics | Surat |
| Hosiery, cycles, auto parts | Ludhiana |
| Engineering, castings, pumps, bearings | Rajkot, Coimbatore |
| Ceramics, tiles, sanitaryware | Morbi |
| Brassware, metal homeware | Moradabad |
| Leather, footwear | Kanpur, Agra, Chennai, Ambur |
| Glass | Firozabad |
| Fireworks, matches, printing | Sivakasi |
| Handicraft, gems, block print | Jaipur |
| Electronics assembly | Noida, Sriperumbudur, Hosur |
| Ayurveda, cosmetics, supplements | Haridwar, Baddi, Ahmedabad |
| Food processing, snacks, spices | Indore, Rajkot, Nashik, Guntur |
| Packaging, corrugation, labels | everywhere; Mumbai has plenty within reach |

**How to find one without introductions:** IndiaMART and TradeIndia for a first list, then
the cluster's own industry association, then the exhibitor list of the relevant trade fair
even if it has already happened. Call ten, visit three. A Mumbai founder can reach Surat,
Morbi and Rajkot in a day each.

**What to ask on the first call**, in this order, because the first answer usually ends it:
minimum order quantity, price at MOQ and at ten times MOQ, sample cost and lead time,
production lead time, whether they already private-label for others, and what certifications
they hold. **MOQ is the number that kills most first products** — if the MOQ is 5,000 units
and the founder has Rs 2 lakh, the idea is over unless you find a smaller unit or a job-work
arrangement.

**Cheaper than manufacturing:** white-label an existing product and put your own brand on it;
job-work, where you supply the design and they run a short batch; or buy finished goods from
a wholesale market, sell them, and only then commit to production.

## Distribution, and what each channel really costs

| Channel | Founder's cut | What it actually takes |
|---|---|---|
| **Own D2C site** | Highest margin | You pay for every visitor. Paid acquisition is usually the reason D2C brands lose money |
| **Amazon / Flipkart** | Marketplace fee, plus fulfilment | Demand already exists; you compete on price and reviews from day one |
| **Meesho** | Lowest fee, lowest price point | Volume at thin margin, price-led buyers |
| **Quick commerce (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart)** | Listing terms are hard, margins thin | Enormous velocity for the right impulse SKU. Getting listed at all is the barrier |
| **General trade distributors** | You keep least | They own the retailer relationship. Realistic for scale, brutal for a first product |
| **Modern trade** | Listing fees, slotting, credit terms | Not a starting channel with limited capital |
| **Direct to business buyers** | Highest margin of all | Slow, but a Mumbai founder can walk into ten in a day |

**The margin chain is the whole business.** Write it out before believing any idea:
landed cost → your price to the channel → channel's margin → what the customer pays.
If the customer price is not competitive once everyone has taken their cut, the idea is
dead no matter how good the product is.

## Software and services

Easier to make, harder to be found. The failure mode inverts: manufacturing risk becomes
distribution risk.

- **Who already has the audience?** Accountants, consultants, distributors, industry
  associations and trade bodies each sit on top of the buyer you want. Reaching them is
  usually cheaper than reaching the buyer directly.
- **What is the unpaid version?** For most Indian small businesses it is WhatsApp, Excel
  and Tally. You are not competing with software; you are competing with a habit that is
  free.
- **Who can say yes alone?** Prefer an owner-operator over anyone who needs a committee.
- **Will they pay monthly?** Subscription is not yet a default reflex for many Indian small
  firms. Annual, or one-time with paid support, often closes faster.

## The execution questions every idea must answer

1. Who makes it, and what is their minimum order or minimum engagement?
2. What does one unit cost landed, and what does the customer pay?
3. Who else takes a cut before the money reaches you?
4. How does the first buyer find out this exists?
5. What is the smallest version that can be sold for real money within eight weeks?
6. What has to be bought before the first rupee comes in — and can the founder afford it?

Question 6 is the one that ends most ideas for a founder with Rs 1–3 lakh. Ask it early.
