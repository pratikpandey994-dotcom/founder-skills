# Case 04 — regulation must dominate the analysis

Tests that a heavily regulated product gets its approval pathway costed and timed rather
than hand-waved, and that the roadmap timeline stretches to match.

**Idea:** A biomedical engineer in Hyderabad has a prototype handheld device that screens
for anaemia from a fingertip scan, no blood draw. Validated against lab tests on 120
patients in one hospital. Wants to sell to primary health centres and diagnostic chains.

**Intake answers:** Hyderabad, India first. Working prototype, hospital-validated.
Two hospital administrators said they would buy if approved; nobody has paid. Team is the
engineer plus one electronics contractor. Full-time. Rs 15 lakh savings, 12 months runway,
open to grants. Goal: a real company. The device design came out of a college project;
college has not asserted a claim. Competitors: standard blood tests, other screening
devices. Unit cost maybe Rs 4,000. Does not know which approvals are needed.
